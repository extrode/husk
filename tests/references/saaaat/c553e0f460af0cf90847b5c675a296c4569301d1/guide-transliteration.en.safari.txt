This guide explains the conventions used. Consonants Most consonants have straightforward mappings. Emphatic letters (pronounced with more force) use dots below. This affects pronunciation and transliteration. Letter Assimilation Example ت (t) at- at-Tawbah ث (th) ath- ath-Thaani د (d) ad- ad-Dahr ذ (dh) adh- adh-Dhuhr ر (r) ar- ar-Rahmaan ز (z) az- az-Zakah س (s) as- as-Salam ش (sh) ash- ash-Shams ص (ṣ) aṣ- aṣ-Ṣughraa ض (ḍ) aḍ- aḍ-Ḍuhaa ط (ṭ) aṭ- aṭ-Tawbah ظ (ẓ) aẓ- aẓ-ẓalam ل (l) al- al-Layl (assimilates to itself) ن (n) an- an-Nur Moon letters (ا، ب، ج، ح، خ، ع، غ، ف، ق، ك، م، ه، و، ي) do NOT assimilate — the al- remains unchanged: al-Qadr, al-Ḩijjah, al-Maghrib. These are marked with dots below in our transliteration: Emphatic (dot below) Plain Notes ṣ (ص) s (س) Both are "s" sounds — ṣ is emphatic ḍ (ض) d (د) Both are "d" sounds — ḍ is emphatic ṭ (ط) t (ت) Both are "t" sounds — ṭ is emphatic ẓ (ظ) z (ز) Both are "z" sounds — ẓ is emphatic Example: In aṣ-Ṣughraa (الصغرى), the ṣ represents ص (emphatic), while in as-Salam (السلام), the s represents س (plain). Typography Notes ḥ, ṣ, ḍ, ṭ, ẓ use Unicode dots below (U+1E25, U+1E63, U+1E24, U+1E6D, U+1E93) ʿ (ayn) uses Unicode left half ring (U+02BF) ' (hamza) uses standard apostrophe Long vowels: aa, ii, uu (no macrons)
