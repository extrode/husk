Below are explanations of key terms, their Arabic origins, and how they're used in this app. Night Divisions Islamic tradition divides the night (from Maghrib to Fajr) into portions for determining prayer times, especially for Tahajjud (night prayer) and Isha deadlines. These divisions are based on hadith recommending prayer in the latter portions of the night. Notation Arabic Transliteration Meaning ½ نصف الليل Nisf al-Layl Midpoint between Maghrib and Fajr. General Islamic midnight. ½* نصف الليل Nisf al-Layl (ba'd al-'Ishaa') Midpoint between the end of your Isha prayer and Fajr. Personal timing for night prayer. ⅓ ثلث الليل Thulth al-Layl One-third point after Maghrib. End of the first third. ⅔ ثلثان الليل Thulthaan al-Layl Two-thirds point. Recommended time for Tahajjud begins. ⁵⁄₆ خُمسُ سُدُس الليل Khums Sudus al-Layl Five-sixths of the night. Late night time marker. The post-Isha midpoint (½ Nisf al-Layl after Isha ends) is personal — it depends on when you actually completed your Isha prayer, not a fixed time. This is useful for planning Tahajjud based on your own prayer schedule. Prayer Times Term Arabic Transliteration Meaning Fajr الفجر al-Fajr Dawn. The beginning of the first light. Dawn prayer. Sunrise الشروق ash-Shurooq Sunrise. Prayer is forbidden during this time. Early Duha الإشراق / الضحى الصغرى al-Ishraaq / aḍ-Ḍuhaa aṣ-Ṣughraa ~15-20 min after sunrise. Minor forenoon prayer. Major Duha الضحى الكبرى aḍ-Ḍuhaa al-Kubraa Mid-forenoon, when sun is high. Major forenoon prayer. Dhuhr الظهر adh-Dhuhr Noon. When the sun passes its zenith. Solar zenith الاستواء al-Istiwaa' Solar noon (sun at highest point). Prayer forbidden briefly before Dhuhr. Asr العصر al-Asr Afternoon prayer. Before sunset قبل الغروب Qabl al-Ghuruub Time just before sunset. Nafl prayer discouraged. Sunset الغروب al-Ghuruub Sunset. Maghrib prayer time begins. Maghrib المغرب al-Maghrib Sunset prayer. Marks the start of a new Islamic day. Isha العشاء al-Ishaa Night prayer. Begins after twilight disappears. Witr الوتر al-Wiṭr Odd-numbered prayer, typically 1 or 3 rak'ah, prayed after Isha. Midnight Distinctions This app distinguishes between three types of midnight: Type Arabic Definition Usage Nisf al-Layl نصف الليل Midpoint between Maghrib and Fajr General Islamic midnight; marks halfway through the night. Post-Isha midpoint نصف الليل (بعد العشاء) Midpoint between your Isha prayer end and Fajr Personal timing for Tahajjud planning Civil midnight — 00:00 local time (Gregorian day boundary) Not an Islamic concept; marks new civil day High-Latitude Rules Far from the equator, around midsummer the sun may not go far enough below the horizon for the usual Fajr and Isha angles. The app then estimates those times with one of these rules: Method Arabic Description Angle-Based حسب الزاوية Uses the same twilight angle, scaled proportionally to the night length. Midnight Rule منتصف الليل Fajr no earlier than the middle of the night; Isha no later than halfway from sunset to Fajr. One-Seventh of Night سُبع الليل Uses 1/7th (~14%) of night duration. Conservative estimate. Islamic Day Concept In Islamic tradition, a day begins at Maghrib (sunset), not at midnight or sunrise. This is why this app shows "Day begins" at the previous day’s Maghrib in the timeline. The Islamic day runs from Maghrib to Maghrib Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, and Maghrib of civil Tuesday all belong to the same Islamic day Isha of civil Tuesday belongs to the next Islamic day (starting at Tuesday’s Maghrib)
